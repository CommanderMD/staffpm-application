# About Eric Armstrong

## The Origin Story

I got an Associates degree in Computer Programming in 2002 — Java, C++, COBOL, RPG. Then life happened. The military, a family, businesses to run. I spent 20 years in IT and entrepreneurship without writing much code.

At 46, I built a live algorithmic trading bot from scratch.

Not a tutorial project. Not a demo. A production system running real money across multiple exchanges, with a webhook API layer, an autonomous agent runtime, a pair inference engine, and safety constraints I designed and implemented myself. It went live. It trades.

That's the origin story. And it's the reason I'm here.

---

## What Those 20 Years Actually Were

While I wasn't writing code, I was building products.

I managed a 20-location healthcare network across 8 counties and diagnosed a bottleneck that was crippling a mandatory EMR upgrade — then designed the fix, secured the funding through USAC, and shipped the upgrade across every site.

I built Tech Smart Inc. from a small technology shop into a multi-product company with infrastructure contracts, government IT services, SaaS products, and an AI agent stack. I defined what to build, why to build it, and how to know if it worked.

I launched a wireless ISP (Cloud Nexus LLC), a real estate technology platform (Rent Smart Inc. / RWP), and an investment holding company (Lion Investors Inc.). Not as a figurehead — as the person doing the architecture, the vendor negotiations, the product specifications, and the roadmap decisions.

I designed two token ecosystems, a five-tier NFT economy, a password manager, a consumer health app, and a blockchain-integrated survival game with a completed Epic Games JV pitch package.

None of that is a job title on a traditional resume. All of it is product work.

## Why I Operate This Way

I'm a systems thinker. I don't see products in isolation — I see how they connect, how they create leverage, how a decision in one layer of the stack creates an opportunity or a constraint in another.

That's the mindset I bring to product management. Not "what feature should we build next" but "what does the system need to be true for this outcome to be possible, and what's the sequence of decisions that gets us there."

It's also why the GitHub role specifically makes sense for me. I'm not applying to manage a product I've read about. I'm applying to help build the platform I use every day to ship my own work — and have strong opinions about where it needs to go.

## The #40built Thing

I operate under the hashtag `#40built` — the idea that building something serious after 40, with everything you've learned and everything you've been through, is its own kind of edge.

I'm not trying to out-hustle a 25-year-old. I'm bringing 20 years of hard lessons, a systems architecture brain, and the discipline of someone who's had to make things work with limited resources in unforgiving environments.
